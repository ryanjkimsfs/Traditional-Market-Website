1 What we build

Traditional markets lose to supermarkets on one thing only: certainty. A shopper cannot see the produce before leaving home, cannot tell whether the stall opened today, and cannot ask a question without walking there. Everything else the market has, price, freshness, the person behind the counter, is intact.

MarketMesh sells that certainty. It is two products in one codebase, and both must work:

The information half

A public, searchable record of every connected market: where it is, when it opens, which stalls trade there, what they sell, and what the counter looked like minutes ago. This works for a visitor who never buys anything, and it is what earns search traffic.

The retail half

Cart, order, per-stall settlement, pickup or same-day delivery. A market order is several small orders that travel together, and the system models it that way rather than pretending a market is one shop.

Six rules that settle arguments later

RULE-1Freshness is stated, never implied. Every stall image carries its exact capture time directly underneath, colour-coded by age. A stall with no photo says so rather than showing a stock image.
RULE-2Detected prices are advisory. A price read off a paper tag is marked an estimate. A merchant-entered price is authoritative. The two are never mixed silently.
RULE-3The system degrades in public. Live camera → today's photo → older photo → nothing registered. Each state gets its own designed treatment.
RULE-4The phone number is a feature. Calling the merchant is a primary action on every stall surface. A purchase that ends in a phone call is a success, not a funnel leak.
RULE-5Location data is real or absent. Coordinates come from an authoritative source with provenance stored. Where a market's internal layout is not published, the interface says the arrangement is illustrative.
RULE-6A market is many businesses. Orders, settlement, hours, closures and stock are per stall. The market is a grouping, never the seller.

4 Merchant

The half that decides whether the platform stays alive. If merchants stop refreshing photos, the shopper product is worthless within a week.

Design constraint

The median merchant is over 60, works with wet hands, and has one phone. The console is judged on one number: seconds from opening the app to a fresh photo being live. Target under 15, camera launch included. Anything that adds a step to that flow gets cut.

ME-1Upload today's photo in three taps, from the phone camera or the gallery. v1 The server stamps the capture time from the image metadata or the moment of receipt. The merchant cannot edit it.
ME-2Upload a short video (up to 30 seconds) as an alternative to a photo, for stalls with no camera. v1 Transcoded server-side to MP4 and a poster frame; plays in the shopper's viewer with the same zoom controls.
ME-3Review detections: accept, correct or remove each item the detector found. v1 Corrections are stored as labelled training data. This is how the model improves.
ME-4Set price, unit and origin per product; one-tap sold out. v1 Origin is a required field, not optional. See §16.
ME-5Order queue with an audible alert; accept, decline with a reason, or adjust the weight before capture. v1
ME-6Open and close for the day with one control, which drives the public status. v1
ME-7Camera status in plain words with a call-support button. v1
ME-8A daily reminder to refresh the photo, by KakaoTalk, at an hour the merchant chooses. v1 Cheapest possible intervention against the platform's main failure mode.
ME-9Payout account, settlement history, monthly statement. v1
ME-10Turn the camera off, temporarily or permanently, without calling anyone. v1 Legal

9 Object detection

The detector turns a counter into a product list. It runs on a snapshot every 20 seconds, not on every frame, and its output is a proposal the merchant can overrule.

DET-1Inference runs on the market gateway, not in the cloud. v1 Sending every snapshot from every camera to a cloud GPU costs more than the platform earns and breaks when the uplink drops. This is the same edge-versus-cloud split already being studied in this repository.
DET-2Runs every 20 s while the stall is open, and immediately when a merchant uploads a photo. v1
DET-3Publish at confidence ≥ 0.80; hold 0.60–0.80 for merchant review; discard below. v1
DET-4Every published item stores its model version, confidence and the exact frame it came from. v1
DET-5Merchant corrections are written back as training data. v1
DET-6A stall with no detections still shows its photo and its product list. Detection enhances, never gates. v1
DET-7Price OCR on the tag crop, always advisory, never used to compute a cart total. v2 Handwritten marker on fluorescent card is the hard case and the common one. Optional, exactly as the brief says.
StageChoiceDetail
ModelYOLO11-s or RT-DETR-R18Detection only; segmentation adds cost without changing the product
Classes60–120 SKU groupsNamed as a shopper names them: 사과, 배, 감귤, 시금치. Not cultivar level
Dataset v13,000–5,000 labelled framesFrom the pilot market's own cameras, morning, midday and dusk

10.1 Getting the data from Naver, legitimately

Naver is where Korean shoppers expect map data to come from, and its place records carry the phone numbers and categories that make a listing complete. Use the official APIs; scraping the map site or bulk-storing its place database breaches the terms and puts the project at risk.

The rule that keeps this clean

Naver seeds and displays; 도로명주소 and VWorld are what you store. Pull a candidate record from Naver to save the association typing, have a human confirm it, then resolve the address through 도로명주소 and the coordinate through VWorld, and store that with its source recorded. Every market in the prototype already carries such a provenance field, and it is what let us catch coordinates that were out by more than a kilometre.

Decide

Map vendor: Naver (most familiar to Korean shoppers, needs NCP keys and per-domain registration, paid at volume), Kakao (similar, strong on 길찾기), or VWorld/OSM through MapLibre (free, no vendor lock, slightly plainer Korean cartography). The prototype runs the third. Deep links out to Naver and Kakao work regardless.

Conventions

  • Errors return { code, message_ko, message_en, field }. The code is stable and machine-readable; the messages are display-ready in both languages.
  • Money is an integer of KRW. No floats anywhere in the money path.
  • Timestamps are RFC 3339 with an offset. The server stores UTC, the client renders KST.
  • Every write accepts Idempotency-Key and returns the original result on replay.
  • Rate limits: search 60/min per IP, stream start 20/min per session, media upload 30/hour per stall, recommendations 30/min per session.

18 Media handling

Merchant uploads are the platform's lifeblood and its most awkward input: an iPhone photo taken with wet hands over LTE.

MD-1Accept HEIC. It is the iPhone default and will be most uploads. Transcode server-side. v1 Rejecting HEIC silently excludes a large share of merchants.
MD-2Read the capture time from EXIF, then strip all EXIF. v1 Legal The timestamp is the freshness contract. The GPS tag in the same block is the merchant's home address if they shot it off-site, and must never reach the CDN.
MD-3Honour EXIF orientation before stripping, or half the photos publish sideways. v1
MD-4Downscale on the device before upload; cap the request at 12 MB. v1
MD-5Publish AVIF and WebP with a JPEG fallback at widths 320 / 640 / 960 / 1440, content-hashed and cached immutably for a year. v1
MD-6Video: 30 s maximum, H.264 MP4 at 720p, poster frame extracted, audio stripped. v1 Audio strips for the same reason cameras have no microphone. See §24.
MD-7Run the detector on every accepted upload and show the merchant the result while they are still in the app. v1
MD-8Keep the previous media until the new one finishes processing, so a stall never goes blank mid-upload. v1
MD-9Face blurring on merchant uploads too, not only on camera frames. v1 Legal

19 Caching & freshness

The hard part of this product is that a page is mostly static except for the one thing that must never be stale. Split the page accordingly.

WhatCacheWhy
Market and stall page shellISR, revalidate 60 sNames, hours and addresses barely change
Freshness, live state, detectionsclient polls 20–30 s, no HTTP cacheThis is the claim the product makes; it cannot be stale
Stall photo filesimmutable, 1 year, content-hashedA new photo is a new URL, so the old one caches forever
Playback URLnever cached, signed, expires in minutesA cached stream URL is a permanent public feed
Search results30 s edge cache on the query stringAbsorbs repeat queries without hiding new stock for long
Map tilesper the vendor's termsNaver and Kakao restrict tile caching. Read the licence
Cart and ordersneverStated so nobody adds a CDN rule that breaks it
The trap

Do not bake the freshness stamp into server-rendered HTML and then cache that HTML. Render the shell, fetch freshness client-side, and the timestamp stays honest at every layer. Vary the cache key on locale, never on the user.

24 Legal gates

Broadcasting video from a public marketplace is the highest-risk thing this platform does. These block launch.

LAW-1Counter-only framing. Cameras point at the merchant's own counter, not down the aisle, and the merchant approves the framing at install. Legal
LAW-2Face blurring on the gateway before any frame leaves the market. Legal
LAW-3No audio. Buy cameras with no microphone. Recording conversation engages 통신비밀보호법. Legal
LAW-4CCTV notices (영상정보처리기기 안내판) at the market entrance and each filmed stall: purpose, scope, retention, contact. Legal
LAW-5Retention limits. Live segments ephemeral, keyframes 30 days at most, nothing archived without a stated purpose. Legal
LAW-6Merchant consent per stall, in writing, with a self-service off switch in the console. Legal
LAW-7원산지 표시. Origin must be displayed for agricultural and fishery products; the platform inherits the duty when it lists them. Legal
LAW-8전자상거래법. 통신판매업 신고, business identity, terms, and an accurate 청약철회 policy including the fresh-food exemptions. Legal
LAW-9개인정보 보호법. Consent, minimum collection, deletion on request, domestic controller of record. Legal
The one that ends the project

A camera aimed down an aisle streams shoppers' faces to the public internet without their consent. That is not a bug report, it is the end. Framing approval and face blurring are therefore install-time gates enforced in the admin tool: a camera that has not passed both cannot be switched public.

25.2 Security

  • Gateways authenticate with mTLS client certificates, rotated on a schedule; revoking one stops ingest immediately.
  • Playback URLs are signed and expire in minutes, so a leaked URL never becomes a permanent public feed.
  • Secrets in a managed store. Never in the repository, never baked into a container image.
  • Payment webhooks verify their signature and are replay-protected.
  • Merchant actions on price, stock, media and orders are audit-logged with actor, before and after.
  • Uploads are content-type sniffed, size capped, and processed outside the web tier.
  • Dependency and container scanning in CI; a failing scan blocks the deploy.
  • Default camera credentials changed at install and recorded as a completed step. See CAM-0.

25.3 Operations

  • Alert on: gateway missing two heartbeats, camera offline 15 minutes, zero detections from a trading stall, payment webhook failures, error rate above baseline.
  • A runbook per alert, written for a non-engineer first responder, because the pilot on-call may not be an engineer.
  • Nightly backups with a restore rehearsed before launch, not after the first incident.
  • Deploys reversible in one command; migrations backward compatible for one release.

The five journeys that must always pass

  • Find a market by area, open a stall, zoom the live view, call the merchant.
  • Search a product by 초성, reach the stall, add it, check out as a guest.
  • Allow location, see the nearest market first, open its layout, tap a stall.
  • Merchant uploads a photo; it is live with a correct timestamp within ten seconds.
  • Merchant accepts an order, adjusts the weight, and the captured amount matches.

A feature is done when

  • It works in Korean and English, at 360 px and on desktop, in light and dark.
  • Its empty, loading and failure states are designed and reachable in staging.
  • It is keyboard operable and passes the contrast check.
  • Its permissions are enforced server-side and covered by an API test.
  • Its analytics events fire.
  • The console is clean and the performance budget still holds.
